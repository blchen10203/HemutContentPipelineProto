Say each thing once. Use each claim at most once, use only one wording per claim, and do not restate a claim's point in your own words elsewhere in the post. Two claims that say nearly the same thing count as one point: pick one. Pick the claims that serve this angle best and leave the rest out.

Before you answer, reread every word you wrote yourself (title, body, stat card label, CTA text; not the placeholders) for the two mistakes the validator rejects most:
- A number word, even as a figure of speech: "half", "twice", "two", "three", "dozen", "hundred" ("not half the product", "a two-step setup", "twice the work"). Say it without the number.
- A plan name: Command Pro and Command All-in appear only inside a claim's wording. In your own words say "the trial", "every plan" or "the whole product".

Build each sentence around the wording, so the wording reads as part of the sentence:
- Good: "Run it on your own loads for {{claim:trial-loads-per-truck}}."
- Bad: a lead-in that ends in a colon, then the wording alone on its own line or as a fragment ("Here's what it covers:", "For context:", "This is not a separate purchase:").
- A wording whose first word is a capitalized ordinary word ("A", "Free", "Every", "No", "Your") starts its sentence or list item; never put it after "with", "and" or other words mid-sentence. Names and acronyms (Command, Hemi, IFTA, ELD) can go anywhere.
- If no wording of a claim fits the sentence you want, write a different sentence or leave the claim out. Never bend the sentence so it reads awkwardly around the wording.
